Give-Up Game Lab

Learning players compete in a waiting contest. Both pay 1 per second, and whoever quits first loses a prize worth 1. The unbeatable strategy is to quit at a random time with a steady chance per second (an exponential law, mean 1). Watch a population of players learn it by trial and error, each with a brain that can be wired like the real fruit-fly give-up circuit.

Arena: one fight at a time

Player 1 fights a random opponent from the population, using the strategies they have learned so far. Every tick (0.127 s of fighting) each fly decides “keep going or leave?” with the give-up chance shown. Deciding step by step like this is exactly the same game as picking a quitting time in advance, so the arena and the charts always agree. The pushing and lunging is only for show: in this game nothing but time decides the winner.

How beatable are the players?

average player whole population, as one mixed strategy where theory says learning settles

Exploitability is how much a perfect opponent could win from you, computed exactly over all 64 possible quitting times. With luck-driven learning it levels off near 0.03 to 0.05 rather than reaching 0. Players also drift apart over time: independent learners tend to specialise, some quitting early and some late, while the group as a whole stays near the ideal. With a fixed pair of 2, one player usually ends up always giving in, a winner/loser split.
